# County Observation Layer — Interpretation Notes

## What the two numbers mean

The dashboard intentionally keeps two different evidence layers visible:

1. **UDELep Texas-range host associations** — the broader host-potential count. This remains the main number for a plant genus.
2. **Observed in the selected county** — the subset of those associated taxa that match research-grade iNaturalist observations in that county.

The county number does not replace the UDELep number.

## Display choices

- **Show UDELep totals + county observations** is the default. It keeps the broader UDELep insect list and labels county observations.
- **Only show county-observed insects** is an optional strict list filter. Even in this mode, plant summaries continue to identify the broader UDELep total separately.

## What county observation evidence does not prove

A research-grade observation shows that a taxon was documented within the iNaturalist county place. It does not by itself prove:

- a resident or breeding population;
- abundance or present-day persistence;
- caterpillar feeding on the searched plant inside that county;
- that an unmatched species is absent.

Observation effort, taxonomic difficulty, date, season, obscured records, and name crosswalks can all affect the county subset.

## Recommended public reading

Read the numbers together. For example:

> **545 UDELep Texas-range host associations**  
> **156 observed in Hays County through research-grade iNaturalist records**

The first is broader potential supported by the host database. The second is a local observation-evidence subset.
